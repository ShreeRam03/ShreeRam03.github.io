Building a phishing email analyzer in Python

During my SOC internship at GreyTheta, a big part of the work was looking at reported emails and deciding: phishing or not? I did it by hand, reading headers and hovering over links. This tool does the same first pass automatically and explains every point it adds to the score.

Why I built it

Phishing triage is repetitive, and the signs are well known: failed sender authentication, a reply address on another domain, a link whose text says one site but points to another, an invoice that is really an .exe. Checking them by hand is slow and easy to get wrong when you have a queue of reports. A script that lists the evidence lets an analyst spend their time on the judgement, not the reading.

What it checks

01AuthenticationSPF, DKIM and DMARC results from the receiving server.
02SenderReply-To or Return-Path on a different domain; brand names in the display name.
03LinksRaw IPs, shorteners, punycode, and link text that hides the real host.
04AttachmentsRisky types, double extensions, SHA-256 for every file.

Each indicator adds points, and the total gives a verdict: low risk, suspicious, or high risk. VirusTotal lookups are optional. The API key is read from an environment variable, never from the code, which is a lesson I took from my website blocker.

def check_links(email):
    for link in email.links:
        host = _domain(link.url)
        # link text shows one site, href goes to another
        if shown and _base_domain(shown) != _base_domain(host):
            findings.append(Finding("Link", f"Link text shows {shown} but goes to {host}", 30))

A real run

I tested it on two sample emails with fake domains: a PayPal look-alike and a genuine college newsletter.

Risk score: 100/100  ->  HIGH RISK - likely phishing   # look-alike
Risk score:   0/100  ->  LOW RISK                      # newsletter
The strongest single signal was the link whose text said www.paypal.com but pointed to a raw IP address. That is exactly the check people skip when they are busy.

What I learned

  • Read HTML, not just text. The deceptive part of a phishing link is the gap between the visible text and the href, so the parser has to read <a> tags.
  • Trust the receiving server's verdict. The tool reads Authentication-Results rather than re-checking SPF itself, because the receiving server saw the real connection.
  • Test with a clean email too. A detector that flags everything is useless. The genuine sample must score low, and it is a unit test.
